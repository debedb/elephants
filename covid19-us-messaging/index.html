All of this is open source. Not just the data — the argument. Nothing about how this page was made is withheld, because an argument you cannot audit is worth less than one you can:

  • The sources — every one linked, on the row, in the open.
  • The criteria — the rule that decides what counts as a source is written down, and this page enforces it in code.
  • The argument — stated in its own section, marked as ours, with the case against it published beside it.
  • The process and the prompts — how entries are verified, and the actual instructions that produced each round of work.
  • The page itself — one file, no build step, editable by anyone.

Disagreement is settled the way open source settles anything: in public, as a change to the thing itself, which is either merged or refused on the record.

Only .gov sources on this page. Every row links to something published by the United States government: an FDA review memorandum, a CDC media statement, an MMWR report, a White House transcript. There is no journalism here, no commentary, and no analysis of either. Where an official said something on television, it is out — see "Sought, and not included" below for what that rule cost us.

The most quoted line in this whole argument is missing, on purpose. On 29 March 2021 the CDC Director said of vaccinated people that "they don't get sick" and "do not carry the virus". She said it on MSNBC. It is not a government publication, so it is not on this page, even though it is the single most useful quotation for the argument we are making.     The argument, stated plainly

    This is ours, and it is the only claim on the page that is. The strongest public assurances were made in the window when the government's own documents did not support them, and they were withdrawn without ever being retracted as claims. The FDA said at authorization that transmission data were limited. Fourteen weeks later the CDC reported a real reduction in infection and framed it as preventing spread. Three months after that a President told a televised audience that vaccinated people were not going to get COVID at all. Six days later the CDC told vaccinated people to put masks back on indoors, and three days after that its Director said in writing that vaccinated people infected with Delta can transmit the virus. By August 2022 the guidance no longer distinguished the vaccinated from anyone else.

    Every one of those steps is defensible on its own. The claim is about the sequence: the confidence of the public message ran ahead of the evidence in the government's own files, and the correction arrived as a guidance change rather than as a correction.

    The strongest objections to it

    These are not strawmen. If we lose the argument, we expect to lose it on one of these.

    • The March 2021 claim was not invented — it was true of the virus at the time. The CDC study found a 90% reduction in all infections, symptomatic and not, against the variants then circulating. Reduced infection really does mean reduced transmission. Delta was a materially different virus with a different immune-escape profile, and a claim that was well supported in March can become false in July without anybody having lied in March.
    • Changing guidance on new evidence is the system working, not failing. The CDC reversed itself within days of receiving unpublished outbreak data and said so in writing. The alternative — holding a position because reversing looks bad — is the actual failure mode. A timeline that reads reversals as embarrassments punishes exactly the behaviour we want.
    • A town hall is not agency guidance. Presidential speech is political persuasion aimed at raising uptake, and holding CDC's documentary record responsible for a President's compression of it conflates two very different institutions. No CDC document ever said "you are not going to get COVID".
    • Cycle threshold values are not transmission. The Barnstable inference — similar viral loads therefore similar infectiousness — was itself a leap beyond what the data showed, made under time pressure. If we credit it here as the moment the story changed, we are accepting a standard of evidence we would not accept from the other direction.
    • Eight documents is a sample, and we picked it. Federal agencies published thousands of pages in this period, including plenty that hedged carefully. A different eight could support a different conclusion.

    Sought, and not included

    Three things belong in this story that are not on the axis above. The rule this page set for itself excluded them, and publishing the exclusions is the only way that rule means anything.

    • "Vaccinated people ... do not carry the virus." The CDC Director, 29 March 2021 — the single most useful quotation for the argument this page makes. She said it on MSNBC. Not a government publication, so it is out.
    • CDC's September 2021 change to its published definitions of "vaccine" and "vaccination" — from wording about producing immunity to wording about producing protection. The current definitions are on cdc.gov; we could not locate the superseded wording on any government host, only in third-party web archives. Under this page's rule we cannot show the before and after, so we do not assert the change at all.
    • A statement by Dr. Fauci, in his own voice, on an NIH domain, about vaccination and transmission. We looked and did not find one. He appears above only as a named signatory of the 18 August 2021 joint statement. If you know of one, that is a pull request.

    How to argue with this page

    You don't have to persuade us. Everything here — every date, every quotation, every word of the argument above — sits in a single public file that anyone is allowed to edit. You change what you think is wrong and send us your version. We either accept it, publicly, or we have to say publicly why not.

    • A quotation is wrong or out of context. The full document is one click away on every row; if the surrounding paragraph changes the meaning, that is the strongest correction you can make and we want it.
    • A federal document is missing. Particularly one that hedged where this page implies confidence. Those are worth more here than ones that help us.
    • A source is not really .gov. That is a bug by this page's own stated rule, and it gets fixed.
    • The argument itself is wrong. Rewrite it. It is the one part of this page we actually wrote.

    And if you'd rather not argue with us at all, take the whole thing. You are free to make a complete copy of this page and publish your own version under your own name, with different documents and a different conclusion. You need nobody's permission. Send us yours and we will link to it from here.
